# After TestFlight

{: .no_toc }

How to help once the build is on your device: what is worth reporting, and how to report it so it can
be fixed.

1. TOC
{:toc}

## Use it with somebody

The most useful thing a tester can do is have a real conversation in it, with a real person, for more
than a day. Almost everything this app does is between two devices, and the faults that matter are
the ones that only appear when a message has to cross.

If you can, do these at least once:

- Start a room and invite somebody, reading the characters to each other out loud.
- Send a photo and a clip, not only words.
- Leave the app closed for a day, then open it and see whether what was sent while you were away
  arrives.
- Put one device in airplane mode, send from the other, and bring it back.

## What is worth reporting

- **Anything that says one thing and does another.** A mark that says delivered when it was not, a
  screen that says a message went when it did not, copy that describes a feature that is not there.
- **Anything you could not undo.** A control that did something permanent without asking first.
- **Anything you could not reach.** A button that did nothing, a screen you could not get back from,
  text you could not read at your text size.
- **Anything that lost something.** A message that vanished, a draft that did not survive, a
  conversation that emptied itself.

A screenshot and the time it happened are worth more than a careful description. If you can say what
you did just before it, say that too.

## How to report it

- **In TestFlight**, take a screenshot and use Apple's own feedback — it carries the build number and
  the device with it, which saves a round trip.
- **Through the site**, at [outpostmessaging.com/contact](https://outpostmessaging.com/contact), for
  anything longer than a sentence or anything you would rather not put in a screenshot.
- **Never send the content of a conversation** to report a defect about it. Nobody needs it, and the
  app is built so that nobody can read it. Describe what happened instead.

## Reporting somebody, rather than something

Reporting abuse is a different path and it is in the app: open the message, report it, and the app
prepares a report that never carries the message or any photo in it. It is described on
[Trust and safety](trust-and-safety.md), and the form it reaches is at
[outpostmessaging.com/report](https://outpostmessaging.com/report).

## What happens to what you send

A defect report is read by a person, which at the moment is one person. There is no queue, no ticket
number and no promise of a reply. What comes of it shows up on [the roadmap](roadmap.md), which is
the status of record for everything in the app.
