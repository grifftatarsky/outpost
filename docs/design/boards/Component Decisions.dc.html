Outpost · design pass · part one

Component decisions

Twelve places where the built app and the design set disagree about a component. Each is ruled here once, with the reason and the consequence. Where the ruling goes against the app, the app changes; where it goes against a board, the board is redrawn in the same pass. Two of the twelve — delivery marks and haptics — had no board behind them at all and are answered as proposals rather than ratified.

Six rulings for the app as built, three for the app with an amendment, one split, one against it, one answered with a third option. The pattern is not a compromise: the app wins wherever it accepted a system behaviour or fixed a real state the board had not considered, and loses wherever it rebuilt something the platform already owns.

01 · Dark ground

The app. Pure black is a hole, not a surface, and on OLED every boundary against it becomes a hard cut with no shading to carry depth. Keeping a raised content surface above a near-black ground also preserves the one structural idea the light mode already uses, so the two appearances read as the same app.

Every dark board is redrawn on the near-black neutral with the surface above it. The ground and the surface both enter the contrast audit as composited pairs, at all seven accents.

02 · Accent wash on the chrome

The app, amended. Making the wash a setting is right, and putting it beside the colour it tints is the only place it can be understood. The amendment is that it must ship off by default and that the washed bars are audited as their own surfaces: a tinted bar changes what every label and separator on it is composited over, which is exactly the pair the floor exists to catch.

Boards draw plain chrome as the default and the washed state as a variant. Appearance gains the switch beside the swatches. Sixteen contrast tests become thirty-two, or the wash is clamped to an opacity that cannot move a pair below the floor.

03 · The sidebar's room count

The app. The system badge owns its own inset, its Dynamic Type behaviour and its accessibility label. A number positioned by hand matches at one type size and drifts at the rest.

Board redrawn to the system badge.

04 · Delivery marks — proposal, amended

Accepted in substance, changed in three particulars. Two states rather than three is the honest count and dots rather than ticks is the right way to avoid borrowing a vocabulary the app cannot honour. But a pair of orbs distinguished by whether they are lit carries its whole meaning in brightness, which is the failure mode rule five exists to prevent: the empty state and the filled state must differ in shape, not only in fill, and each needs a spelled-out VoiceOver string rather than a count.

The second particular is the wording. The second mark reports that a recipient's device said it had displayed the message, which is not the same claim as a person having read it, and the label has to say the smaller thing. The third is that reporting reading at all is a disclosure the reader makes about themselves, so it is opt-in, and a sender looking at a mark that will never light needs to be able to tell that from a message that has not arrived.

New board: the mark in all its states, at three type sizes, with its labels. App changes the unlit mark to an outline, adds the two accessibility strings, and adds the read-reporting switch with a distinct third state for "not reported".

05 · Reacting to a post

Split. The trigger goes to the app: a visible smiley on the reaction row is discoverable, reachable under VoiceOver and does not compete with the system's own long-press menus, all of which the drawn hold gesture forfeits. Recents-first stays as drawn. The fixed emoji list goes against the app — a hand-maintained list is a permanent liability that ages every time the Unicode set does, and a plus that opens the system picker is what somebody who has never seen the app already expects.

Board redrawn with a tap trigger. App replaces the fixed list behind the plus with the system emoji picker.

06 · "Visible to no one yet."

A third option. The app is right that an empty audience is not a failure and should not be narrated under the composer on a first-run screen. But deleting the line leaves a real question unanswered — somebody typing into their Outpost on day one does not know whether they are writing into a void or broadcasting to a room. The answer belongs in the audience rail, stated as a fact about who will collect this rather than as a warning about who cannot.

Board 15's audience rail gains its empty state. The composer line stays deleted.

07 · Bubble radius

The app. A fixed radius on a one-line bubble is a lozenge, and the design set never drew the short case to notice. Clamping to the bubble's own height is the standard fix and holds at every type size.

Boards redrawn with one-line, two-line and accessibility-size bubbles side by side, so the clamp is visible in the set rather than implied.

08 · Disabled buttons

The app. A dimmed accent is still the accent; it says "primary action" while refusing to behave as one, and it does so differently at each of the seven accents. A neutral fill says unavailable once, identically, everywhere.

Boards redrawn. The disabled label still needs to be legible against the neutral fill — dimming the fill is not licence to drop the text below the floor.

09 · Text fields

The app. Fields were being missed as tappable, which is the only evidence that settles this. An explicit fill and border also give the field an edge the contrast floor can measure, where the light treatment gave it nothing to test.

Boards redrawn. Field borders enter the audit as interface edges at 3:1.

10 · Naming a room, and composing

The app. An alert's field is one line, cannot scroll and cannot hold the sentence a room name sometimes needs, let alone a paragraph. A sheet is the system component for the job and brings its own dismissal, keyboard avoidance and large-type behaviour.

Boards redrawn as sheets. This folds into the new-room board owed in Part 2.

11 · Custom headers on the tab roots

Against the app. This is the most expensive divergence in the table and the one worth reversing. A hand-drawn header gives up large titles and their collapse on scroll, the toolbar's own Dynamic Type and accessibility behaviour, the search field's placement, and everything the OS adds to navigation bars in the next release — for one person to maintain, on three platforms, forever. The stated reasons do not hold up: consistency across three roots is what the system bar gives by construction, and the sync line does not need to live inside the bar. Attach it as a top safe-area inset directly beneath the bar, where it can be its own row, wrap at accessibility sizes, and disappear when there is nothing to say.

App changes: three tab roots move to system navigation bars with large titles, sync line as an inset row below. Boards keep the system bar they already drew and gain the sync row as a separate element.

12 · Haptics — vocabulary, not ratification

Accepted, bounded to three cues. A named vocabulary is the right instinct; what it was missing is a rule about what a cue is allowed to mean. Haptics here never carry information on their own — each one accompanies a visible change that says the same thing, so the app remains fully usable with the system's haptics off, which it must be.

Three cues, and no fourth without a board. Commit, on an action that cannot be undone from this screen — sending, joining, leaving, removing — a single light impact. Refusal, when the app declines something the person asked for, paired with the message that says why: a warning notification. Failure, only for a send that will not go, paired with the inline failure above the composer: an error notification. Arrival of a message is not a haptic; that is the notification system's job and duplicating it makes the app buzz twice for one event.

No board needed, but the vocabulary is written into the design set as a note beside the send and leave boards. App drops any cue not on this list.

Part two · rulings taken while drawing

Twenty-two new boards, in turns 6 to 9 of the canvas. Six decisions had to be made to draw them, and they belong in the record for the same reason the twelve above do.

Outpost access is an allow-list defaulting to nobody. Joining a room grants nothing. A new room raises a non-blocking prompt in the conversation — a row, not a sheet, because with the default at no access there is nothing urgent to interrupt for — and the review it opens offers per-person choice with blanket allow and blanket deny as shortcuts through the same rows rather than as a separate mode. Anyone who already had access keeps it and is shown with where it came from. The old room-shaped grant is gone.

Notification levels are four, shown as banners. Everything stays the default. The ladder is drawn as the banner each rung produces rather than as a description, and the screen states the fact that changed since the board was first drawn: the banner is composed on the receiving device after a local decrypt, so nothing readable crosses the network, but the finished banner enters the OS notification store, which the app cannot reach. A per-room override names the default it is following instead of reading as unset.

The welcome tour keeps its promise. The final panel's sentence about finding this again stays, and the affordance it names is drawn: a question mark in the navigation bar of every tab root, opening a how-this-works page that holds the five panels as sections plus the questions people actually arrive with. Cutting the sentence would have been cheaper and left the app with no help at all.

Show hidden messages is a count, not a list. Naming what was hidden undoes the hiding for anyone reading over a shoulder, so restoring is all-or-nothing per room and the messages return to their original positions in the log. Hidden messages are also excluded from local search.

Blocking and reporting are screens, not systems. Blocking reuses the key-handover mechanism and separates its two effects on the screen — you stop collecting from them, they stop being handed your keys — while stating the limit plainly: they remain in the room and everyone else keeps receiving them. Reporting composes an email from the reporter's own device and shows the entire attachment before it goes, because there is no server to report to and none will be added.

Direct messages are separate by default, blended by switch. Boards 51–54 held two proposals the brief called mutually exclusive, and they were — as tab arrangements. Neither needed discarding. A direct message and a twelve-person room are different enough that most people want them in different places, so separate is the default: a Messages tab and a Rooms tab, four tabs in the dock. Merging them is a preference rather than a better answer, so it is a switch, and turning it on gives one Messages list with three tabs. The tab is called Messages in both arrangements, which is the naming that makes the switch legible — nothing is renamed when it is flipped, one list is simply absorbed into the other. In the merged list a group is marked twice and quietly: a second disc behind the avatar, and the sender's name in the preview, which a direct message never carries because there is only one person it could be from.

Board 12 loses its pairing half, and fingerprints gain an ongoing case. The device list stays and is now a record rather than a control. Two screens were owed and are drawn: comparing a member's fingerprint again at any time, dated against the last check, and being told a member's fingerprint changed — presented as an event with both readings in order of likelihood, since the app cannot distinguish a reinstall from an impersonation and should not pretend to. The multi-step leave in turn 2 is confirmed as still wanted; the recovery and lock flows are confirmed as intended. Storage and retention remains blocked behind media.

Appendix · the default accent

Not a divergence, but decided in the same pass and recorded here because every board depended on it. The shipped default moves from cobalt to verdigris — dark value #34A79B, light value #17877C — and the whole design set is retinted to match, so the set no longer shows one accent while the app opens on another. Cobalt remains one of the seven.

App changes its default. All boards retinted. The contrast floor is not unaffected, and the earlier version of this note wrongly said it was: white text on the verdigris base measures 2.94:1, second-worst of the seven, where the outgoing cobalt was 3.88:1 — so the default moved onto a worse pairing. Neither figure ever cleared 4.5:1, which is the finding below.

Where each ruling is now drawn

Turn 10 of the canvas carries the rulings that needed drawing rather than describing. Ruling 1 is applied set-wide instead: all 83 dark screens moved off pure black onto the near-black ground, so the whole set is consistent in one pass rather than board by board.

Board 85 draws the system navigation bar with the sync line as its own row beneath it (11). Board 86 is the delivery-mark specimen — four states, three type sizes, the two spoken strings (4). Board 87 puts the radius clamp, the neutral disabled fill and the bordered field on one sheet (7, 8, 9). Board 88 is the reaction row with its visible trigger and the plus opening the system picker (5). Board 89 is the tinted-bar variant beside the switch that drives it (2). Board 90 is the audience rail in both states, which is where the deleted composer line went (6). Board 26 is the member removal that gated a submission — redrawn rather than drawn: an earlier version existed and stated only the forward-only stop, so it is retired to an unlabelled alternative and the survivor keeps the 26 order token and carries all three facts.

Boards 91 and 92 define light mode as values rather than a second set of drawings: four surfaces, the ink and edge steps, and the accent's light measurement — verdigris #17877C, since the dark #34A79B does not clear 4.5:1 on white. Board 92 puts those values on a real screen to check they hold. Nothing about any layout changes between appearances, which is what makes a second full set unnecessary.

13 · Solid accent fills, found while measuring

Not in the original table, and the most consequential thing this pass turned up. Every filled primary button and every sent bubble in the set puts white text directly on the accent's base value, and no accent base is dark enough to carry it: cobalt 3.88, oxblood 4.33, aubergine 3.36, hangar slate 3.04, verdigris 2.94, olive drab 2.53, signal amber 2.50. Verdigris's light value reaches 4.38. Not one clears 4.5:1, and at 17pt regular and 17pt semibold this is normal text, not large text, so 4.5:1 is the applicable floor rather than 3:1. Sixteen tests are reported green, which means the audit is not currently compositing white-on-accent-fill as a text pair — the pairing was failing before this pass and is failing at every accent, not only the new default.

The fix keeps the accent recognisable rather than replacing it. A solid fill behind white text uses a darkened step of the same accent, derived per accent by taking the base down until white clears 4.6:1; the accent's own value keeps carrying text, icons, tints, edges and the unread dot, where 3:1 is the floor that applies and every accent already passes. This is one token rather than seven hand-picked hexes, so it holds if an accent is ever retuned, and it applies identically in light mode with the light value as its input.

Applied across the set: 77 dark fills and 7 light fills repainted, in every case an element carrying white text. The destructive fill had the identical problem and takes the identical treatment — white on the system red measures 3.41:1, so the three buttons using it (remove from room, cancel the erase, the leave swipe) move to a darkened red at 4.86:1. App adds the derived fill step and uses it for filled buttons and sent bubbles. The audit gains white-on-fill as a text pair at 4.5:1 across all seven accents in both appearances — the test that would have caught this.

What the app has to change

Four items. The three tab roots move to system navigation bars with the sync line as an inset row (11). The plus in the reaction picker opens the system emoji picker instead of a fixed list (5). The unlit delivery mark becomes an outline, gains its two accessibility strings, a third "not reported" state, and read reporting becomes opt-in (4). Any haptic cue outside the three named above is removed (12). The default accent becomes verdigris (appendix).

Two items add tests rather than code. The washed bars are audited as their own surfaces at all seven accents (2), and field borders join the audit as interface edges (9).

Everything else is a board redraw, and the redraws are not cosmetic: the dark ground, the radius clamp, the neutral disabled fill and the filled field are all states the set never drew, which is why the app had to decide them alone.
